Open Threads & the Endgame

☾
Live campaign state — current through Night 20. The one page here that is not module prep but the actual running dossier: what is open, who is moving against whom, and where it all points. Update it as play advances.

The party is Level 7, allied with the Ghost of March, and has brought Mira — the one woman who can cripple the Haseki Sultan — safely into Constantinople and hidden her, invisible, in the dead Osman's house, changing the plan they left Night 19 with. The city hunts her: the Sultan's own distress at her dampened power is driving raids across the bathhouses, and the Padishah is turning over every stone. Now the Ghost has called a Grand Negotiation — four days to gather the city's powers to one table and decide its fate — while behind it all a crowd of loose ends still runs: a hidden Lodge sanctum, a father's smuggling debt, a Widow turned against them, a wet-nurse under the spymaster's eye. This is the ledger of everything still in play.

State of play

The Cabal (Level 7): Gizem Faruki (dervish monk), Miray (druid), Jean-Zera Mongé (bard), Sidra al-Najjar — now a djinn-master warlock since her djinn Faravi was torn loose. Alliance: the Ghost of March / Alemdar. Now sheltering Mira — the Sultan's fugitive sister, newly won over — hidden (and able to turn invisible at will) in Osman's house, with Ashraf the tortoise for company. The Night-19 deaf hours have cleared.

The main quest — Mira found

  • Reached and won over (Night 19). The party found Mira at her farm near the Trojan dig site, defended her against a second Bostanji force, and — through Jean-Zera, Osman's journal, and the shared dreams — earned her fragile trust. She is a powerful, frightened diviner: illusion and decoy-doubles, teleportation of others, blur, an evil-eye curse that struck Sidra mute, and frequent spell-rebounds (one of which deafened Jean-Zera and Gizem). Handle her as fragile and dangerous both.
  • The royal death-warrant. The captured captain bore a sealed, formal writ — not an arrest order but a warrant to take Mira and execute her, under the authority of the Padishah himself. Whether this is Mejid's true will or his seal turned to another's purpose is the campaign's next hinge — and it makes the "bring her to the city" path far more perilous.
  • The Living Manor. Her house is a gargantuan animate construct that fought intruder and rescuer alike, and is bound to its ground: it will not leave, but is compelled to give chase if Mira is stolen from more than 500 ft. Left behind at the farm. (Stats & the encounter: Mira & the Living Manor.)
  • What she confirmed. Osman visited her, was kind, took his notes in that journal, urged her to stay on the farm and seek no one, warned she is a danger to all near her, and swore to protect her only so long as she stayed — the matter-and-antimatter interference that dampens Bergüzar's power the nearer Mira draws. She does not believe her sister wants her dead, but knows others would kill her to profit the Haseki Sultan; whether Bergüzar is complicit is a separate, open question.
  • The sisters' rift. As children, their father raised a hand to Karia (Bergüzar's birth name); their mother killed him with a clock — Karia having magically bent her arm to strike the harder. Blame fell on Karia; Mira was left behind, resentful, when Karia was taken away for training. Mira is not seeking reunion and is markedly non-committal about it. She is the untrained sister; Karia had the schooling.
  • Where she is now (Night 20): the party changed the plan. Rejecting the Undercity bathhouses (too many palace spies) and the Ghost's quarter (too near Kore/Shehrazat), they brought Mira into the city invisible and hid her at Osman's all-but-empty house — few eyes, and a library worth ransacking. Jean-Zera then found a better hiding place still inside it (see the Lodge sanctum, below). Still open: a secret meeting of the sisters out of the Padishah's sight, and — long term — proper training (a university). Alexander's ward Shehrazat remains a parallel case and possible ally. Live risk: the nearer Mira sits to the city, the more Bergüzar's power thins — and her resulting distress is now visibly rippling through the palace and driving the raids.

The Grand Negotiation — called (four days out)

The near-term spine. In the çarşı, the Ghost of March intercepted Sidra (moments after she trailed a cart of his to his compound), signalled that he knew they had Mira — and wanted no details — and warned the Padishah's search is closing the window. His ask: carry his invitation to a Grand Negotiation in four days, at a neutral, well-kept tavern that passes for derelict, to "decide the fate of the city." He named the guests he wants brought: Ahra, Hano, Giannis, Chique Manu, and Sharif. Open: who invites whom, who actually comes, and what the party wants out of it — this is the module's Grand Negotiation beat arriving early, and it sets the endgame table.

The Lodge of Byzantia — its sanctum found

Osman signed his letter Loremaster of the Lodge of Byzantia, and the party already held the Lodge's address, new-moon meeting time, and two secret gestures (via Faravi). Night 20 opened the door further. Exploring Osman's house, Jean-Zera found a false-wall closet holding a magical book that projects the reader into a "study within a study" — a timeless chamber ringed with ancient scrolls (hieroglyphs; a ~1,500-year Aramaic Book of Daniel) and a great round table set with thirteen chairs: the Lodge's own sanctum. A winged, radiant initiate-figure asked, "Are you here to walk with me up the steps of understanding?" — a call-and-response Jean-Zera muffed; it withdrew with "come to me when you are ready." The book can be carried out of the closet, and the party is eyeing it as both a hiding place for Mira and a safe table to meet at. Still unopened: a blind ~6×6 ft walled space by Osman's kitchen that accounts for nothing.

Claude Mongé & the smuggling debt

Claude Mongé, Jean-Zera's father, was at Osman's when they arrived. By burning the Giannis pipeline, Jean-Zera froze Claude's antiquities smuggling; Claude gave him two nights to deliver the crypt relics (done — his hired men crated and shipped them, the Petrus Gyllius diary slipped out with the cargo) and to make good on a pivot to a legal line: officially sanctioned removal of antiquities via a direct connection to the Padishah, with Jean-Zera's sketchbook as catalogue. Claude sails in ~2 nights. An obsequious peace-letter to Giannis came back unopened — that bridge stays burned.

Loose dangers

Things left alive, loose, or unresolved that can resurface:

  • The half-freed Shahmeran. Two of four chains broken; mobile in her cell and able to cast, her "obligation" declared fulfilled. Contact runs through Faravi — an ancient serpentess now has a line into Sidra's own bond.
  • The palace mole. An unnamed official fled the peace talks after Kavalan's murder of Hamit; both the party and Mejid noticed.
  • Kavalan's leverage. He killed Hamit publicly and cast Speak with Dead — putting Jean-Zera's description on record and implicating the palace. Talks continue, strained.
  • Alexander's vendetta. He knows Ahra killed his wife Nadia for using magic. Set aside for now, but a powder keg under the Janissary–Sekban mediation the Ghost wants.
  • Kore's true nature — a Darkness-weaving shade — remains unknown to most of the party.

Faction pressure

  • The Ghost / Alemdar has escalated from "broker Sekban–Janissary unity through Ahra" to calling the Grand Negotiation outright (above) — he wants all the city's powers at one table in four days. His older ask still stands underneath it (unity behind a show of enmity), and Ahra's kill-the-casters creed still cuts against the pluralist end he wants. Note also: he is stockpiling Greek fire — Sidra traced a cart of it from Papa Mert straight to his compound. How the Negotiation goes shapes whether Act Four's last fight is against Ahra alone or against Alemdar himself.
  • Hano "the Widow" has turned. Sought as an old ally, she proved to have her own politics: she favours a hard-handed, anti-magic, anti-non-human captain named Murad over the Padishah, and Miray read a standing note on her desk ordering another hunter to watch — and kill — a past associate suspected of being non-human "the moment they become an issue," a fate framed uncomfortably like Miray's own. Hano suspects Miray is older than she looks but has not yet clocked her as non-human. Treat Hano as a probable opponent at (or after) the Negotiation.
  • A Certain Community / Aziz Sefa is the live antagonist: entangled with the party from Yedikule, implicated in the library-looting, and now openly hunting Mira. Aziz fog-ringed away from the Night 18 ambush and was not present at the manor; the Night 19 attackers were a fresh Bostanji column with Community agents attached, bearing the royal writ. This was already the second wave out of Constantinople, and — the party has realised — more are coming. Aziz will surface again, and next time he knows the party has Mira.

Side errands still open

  • Crown of Sargon — claimed (Nights 17–18) and now attuned to Sidra (at the cost of the prior 24 hours' memory). Live decision: keep it as power in hand, or spend it to buy the loyalty of the cats of the Council of the Ninth Life — who could return palace-spies and running interference. Its ties to the Ninth Life and the sick-cats web remain.
  • Alexander & Shehrazat — Alexander agreed to tutor Shehrazat if hidden from both Ahra and Alemdar; his short-term lodging at Papa Mert's has likely lapsed and needs a longer arrangement. Shehrazat was also told to seek Dr. Feuerbach at the Pera Palace Hotel for her unstable magic, but cannot go above ground herself.
  • The Spectral Hippodrome — Susanna and Chique Manu's race-fixing; a promised new race pending; the Blue Rider's departure has destabilized the Blue/Green balance.
  • The sick cats & red eggs — Sharif's unanswered request, knotted to Eve, the Ninth Life, and the Crown.
  • Giannis the smuggler — the tie stays publicly burned: Jean-Zera's peace-letter came back unopened. Claude's legal Padishah-line pivot (above) is now the party's route for antiquities instead.
  • Deniz — met and half-won (Night 20). Miray found the goat-druid fighting in the Ermeydani, hailed her in Druidic, and reached a tentative accord: balance without subjugation or revenge. Deniz cares only for her oasis and is indifferent to who rules — unless a victor threatens the Undercity, which Eve does (she asked, pointedly, if Miray was "one of Eve's"). A first druid ally, and a live line on the Eve thread.
  • A friend inside the palace — Emine the wet-nurse (alias "Rena"). Referred to Gizem by Gülbahar, she is a covert, frightened Sufism-seeker who wants gentle wisdom to ease another distressed, pregnant lady of the harem (very possibly Bergüzar). Gizem's harem "in" runs through the pregnant concubine Gulnaz and the coffee-mistress Dilrûba. Danger: a spymaster's man tailed Emine home — A Certain Community is watching her.

Where it all points

Mira is reached, won, and now hidden; the near arc has collapsed into the Ghost's Grand Negotiation, four days out — where the party must decide what Mira is for (a shield for the Haseki Sultan, a lever against her, or a fugitive kept quietly alive) even as they gather the city's powers to one table. This is the module's Grand Negotiation beat, arriving early: the succession is decided and the party sets the endgame table — now complicated by a royal warrant that may or may not be the Sultan's true will, and by a Widow (Hano) and a strongman (Murad) pulling the other way. From there the module drives toward The Eyes of Suleiman: the Ghost's plan is to sever Mejid from the Harem by removing Bergüzar, but her visions — and now Mira herself, the sister who could talk her down or take her place — give the party the chance to propose sparing her instead, the campaign's moral hinge. Then the Lunar Eclipse and one of the three finales (see The Acts). On the current alliance, that means clearing the Janissary Remnants and, at the very last, a reckoning with Alemdar — unless the mediation with Ahra, or a change of heart, rewrites the ending.

☾ Next Session (proposed)

☾
Proposal for Night 21 — the Grand Negotiation (and the run-up to it). The clock reads four days. A split-party session: the cabal fans out to gather (or refuse) the Ghost's named guests and to decide what they even want from the table, then the Negotiation itself convenes. Options, not rails — the party sets the guest list and the agenda; let the city's factions collide.

The run-up — delegation (who invites whom)

  • The guest list is a decision, not a chore. The Ghost named five — Ahra, Hano, Giannis, Chique Manu, Sharif — but two are now problems: Hano has turned (do they invite a probable enemy, or leave her out and let her move against them unseen?), and Giannis is burned (the peace-letter bounced; who, if anyone, can approach him?). Let each PC carry an invitation that fits: Gizem/Jean-Zera to Ahra, the Undercity pair to Sharif, Chique Manu the easy yes. Each is a scene, and a chance to read where that faction will stand.
  • Protect the hideout. Mira stays invisible at Osman's; the party wanted wards and checkpoints. The Lodge book is the stronger play — a pocket study no searcher can reach — but stepping into it means facing the initiate-figure again (see below). Decide before the Negotiation whether Mira sits in the book or the bedroom.
  • Buy the cats first? The Crown-vs-Ninth-Life decision wants making before the table: trading the Crown to Sharif's cats could hand the party palace-spies and interference exactly when the Padishah's search is tightening.

The table itself — the Grand Negotiation

  • The fault line. Pluralists (the Ghost, Sharif, the Undercity, a won-over Deniz) against the anti-magic bloc (Hano and her strongman Murad, and whatever of Ahra's kill-the-casters creed survives). The party is the swing. What do they want out of it — the succession settled, Bergüzar spared, Mira kept safe, the Undercity left alone?
  • The Ghost's agenda vs. the moral option. Alemdar's endgame is to sever Mejid from the Harem by removing Bergüzar. With Mira now in hand — the sister who could talk her down or take her place — the party has the lever to propose sparing her instead. The Negotiation is where that case gets made or lost.
  • Wildcards at the door. A Certain Community is watching the palace and the party both; a "derelict" tavern is a fine place for an ambush or an uninvited guest. Play Hano/Murad's people, an Aziz agent, or the warrant itself surfacing as a bargaining chip.

Threads to pull

  • The warrant. Still the central mystery — is the death-order truly Mejid's, or a faction abusing his seal? Gizem's harem appeal is out, and Emine is now a live channel inward (handle with care — she is watched, and could be a mole risk or a victim).
  • The sister-meeting. Lay the ground for a secret Mira–Bergüzar meeting; the Negotiation may be the very cover that lets it happen.
  • The Lodge initiation. Jean-Zera muffed the call-and-response; give him a way to learn the right answer (the scrolls, Osman's papers, Faravi) and return to the angel. The reward is the Lodge itself.
  • Colour & consequence. Claude sails in two nights (a clean exit, or a complication if his relics draw eyes); the blind kitchen wall at Osman's still unopened; the moral weight of hiding a person who is also a weapon.

Where it should land: the Negotiation convened with the party's chosen guests, a stance taken on the succession and on Bergüzar, and Mira still safe. If the party stalls, the Padishah's search tightens and the Sultan's worsening distress escalates the bathhouse raids until the Undercity itself is at risk.
