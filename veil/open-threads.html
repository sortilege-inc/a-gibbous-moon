Open Threads & the Endgame

☾
Live campaign state — current through Night 21. The one page here that is not module prep but the actual running dossier: what is open, who is moving against whom, and where it all points. Update it as play advances.

The party is Level 7, leaning to the Ghost of March as the least-bad of three futures, and still hiding Mira — the one woman who can cripple the Haseki Sultan — invisible in the dead Osman's house. The Grand Negotiation has been held, and it changed the terms: the Ghost's coup is not a proposal but a thing already in motion, to be effected by his Sekban with the table or without it. Three irreconcilable banners now stand plain — the Ghost's pluralist coup toward an assembly, Ahra's anti-magic monarchist coup toward an oligarchy under a pretender, and the Padishah's tightening status quo — and the party swore to none of them, carrying the ideals out the door before the room turned to conspiracy. Behind it all the loose ends still run: a half-mapped Lodge, its sanctum built by a mint assayer; a Widow and a strongman left uninvited and unwatched; a wet-nurse under the spymaster's eye; and the palace itself, waiting on what the party finally decides Mira is for. This is the ledger of everything still in play.

State of play

The Cabal (Level 7): Gizem Faruki (dervish monk), Miray (druid), Jean-Zera Mongé (bard), Sidra al-Najjar — now a djinn-master warlock since her djinn Faravi was torn loose. Alliance: the Ghost of March / Alemdar — the party's declared lean after the Negotiation, though they refused to bless his coup. Still sheltering Mira — the Sultan's fugitive sister — hidden (and able to turn invisible at will) in Osman's house, with Ashraf the tortoise for company. They chose not to bring her to the table.

The main quest — Mira found

  • Reached and won over (Night 19). The party found Mira at her farm near the Trojan dig site, defended her against a second Bostanji force, and — through Jean-Zera, Osman's journal, and the shared dreams — earned her fragile trust. She is a powerful, frightened diviner: illusion and decoy-doubles, teleportation of others, blur, an evil-eye curse that struck Sidra mute, and frequent spell-rebounds (one of which deafened Jean-Zera and Gizem). Handle her as fragile and dangerous both.
  • The royal death-warrant. The captured captain bore a sealed, formal writ — not an arrest order but a warrant to take Mira and execute her, under the authority of the Padishah himself. Whether this is Mejid's true will or his seal turned to another's purpose is the campaign's next hinge — and it makes the "bring her to the city" path far more perilous.
  • The Living Manor. Her house is a gargantuan animate construct that fought intruder and rescuer alike, and is bound to its ground: it will not leave, but is compelled to give chase if Mira is stolen from more than 500 ft. Left behind at the farm. (Stats & the encounter: Mira & the Living Manor.)
  • What she confirmed. Osman visited her, was kind, took his notes in that journal, urged her to stay on the farm and seek no one, warned she is a danger to all near her, and swore to protect her only so long as she stayed — the matter-and-antimatter interference that dampens Bergüzar's power the nearer Mira draws. She does not believe her sister wants her dead, but knows others would kill her to profit the Haseki Sultan; whether Bergüzar is complicit is a separate, open question.
  • The sisters' rift. As children, their father raised a hand to Karia (Bergüzar's birth name); their mother killed him with a clock — Karia having magically bent her arm to strike the harder. Blame fell on Karia; Mira was left behind, resentful, when Karia was taken away for training. Mira is not seeking reunion and is markedly non-committal about it. She is the untrained sister; Karia had the schooling.
  • Where she is now (Night 20): the party changed the plan. Rejecting the Undercity bathhouses (too many palace spies) and the Ghost's quarter (too near Kore/Shehrazat), they brought Mira into the city invisible and hid her at Osman's all-but-empty house — few eyes, and a library worth ransacking. Jean-Zera then found a better hiding place still inside it (see the Lodge sanctum, below). Still open: a secret meeting of the sisters out of the Padishah's sight, and — long term — proper training (a university). Alexander's ward Shehrazat remains a parallel case and possible ally. Live risk: the nearer Mira sits to the city, the more Bergüzar's power thins — and her resulting distress is now visibly rippling through the palace and driving the raids.

The Grand Negotiation — held (Night 21)

The party spent four days filling the Ghost's table, then sat at it. Who they brought: Ahra (invited by Gizem, came late, hostile, armed men in the hall); Sharif and his cats; Chique Manu (transactional as ever); Kore as a voice of the Undercity (a fervent second to the Ghost); Papa Mert (there for Sidra alone); Sadullah Efendi for the faithful; and Bedros Kuyumcuyan, the mint assayer and Lodge remnant, as Jean-Zera's guest. Who they left out: the turned Hano and her strongman Murad (deliberately uninvited — and so unwatched), and the burned Giannis. Mira they kept hidden and unshown.

What the table revealed: the Ghost's coup is not a proposal but a fact in motion — his Sekban are prepared to effect it with the party or without them. His platform, laid out plainly: freedom of movement for the non-human (disguised above, free below); abolition of the Janissaries; the Sultan subdued not destroyed; soft-power abroad; and a military coup followed by an assembly of the people, army-defended and underwritten by the wealthy. Ahra's counter, arriving with her: the non-human confined below; the Janissary Hearth restored; Bergüzar's head on a spike and the Harem dissolved; a hard hand abroad; and the Padishah replaced by a strong pretender she named — Bayezid. Three poles, now fixed: the Ghost (pluralist coup → assembly), Ahra (anti-magic monarchist coup → oligarchy under Bayezid), the Padishah (status quo, tightening on magic).

Where the party landed: they made a pluralist stand (Miray and Jean-Zera both), held their noses in a straw-poll of leanings and chose the Ghost as the least-bad — he alone is not the enemy of magic, and for the common man little would change — but they would not bless a violent coup, and excused themselves before they were made complicit. Sadullah and Papa Mert left with them; Bedros stayed to follow up. No coalition was sworn. The near arc now shifts from "set the table" to what the party is actually for, with a coup already rolling underneath them.

The Lodge of Byzantia — its sanctum found, its roster half-mapped

Osman signed his letter Loremaster of the Lodge of Byzantia, and the party already held the Lodge's address, new-moon meeting time, and two secret gestures (via Faravi). Night 20 opened the door further. Exploring Osman's house, Jean-Zera found a false-wall closet holding a magical book that projects the reader into a "study within a study" — a timeless chamber ringed with ancient scrolls (hieroglyphs; a ~1,500-year Aramaic Book of Daniel) and a great round table set with thirteen chairs: the Lodge's own sanctum. A winged, radiant initiate-figure asked, "Are you here to walk with me up the steps of understanding?" — a call-and-response Jean-Zera muffed; it withdrew with "come to me when you are ready." The book can be carried out of the closet, and the party is eyeing it as both a hiding place for Mira and a safe table to meet at. Still unopened: a blind ~6×6 ft walled space by Osman's kitchen that accounts for nothing.

Night 21 named the members. Ashraf recalled nine besides Osman from the last meeting he attended (decades gone), and Jean-Zera's two days in Osman's correspondence narrowed them:

  • Bedros Kuyumcuyan — the Armenian "up from the mint," now known to be an assayer (he warrants coin is true metal at true worth). Osman's letters to him were coded and contractor-like; the most recent (~2.5 yrs ago) pressed for "the accessibility of the room" — read as the sanctum, built or altered by his magical hand. Attended, and stayed on after the party left.
  • Magus Chartophylax — heir of the Fenariote Orthodox widow who was the Lodge's "walking encyclopaedia." Osman corresponded with the family from the turn of the century; the magus inherited amid "challenging discoveries amongst the mother's effects." A lead, not yet a face — and one likelier for Gizem than for anyone else.
  • The Warden — a heavy-set Egyptian of middle years whose trade ran on great quantities of wax. Name unknown.
  • A young Frenchman — slim, late twenties at the time; unclear whether a true initiate or a guest sent by a kindred Continental order.

The fracture: the Lodge is all but dead. It split at the greatest good — misplaced funds, a spouse gone astray, a member using long-forbidden methods — and the factions no longer speak. Osman, Bedros, and one other stood as neutral brokers who could not mend it. Bedros agreed to try to draw other elements of the Lodge back in; whether the thirteen chairs can ever be filled again is open.

Claude Mongé — sailed

Claude Mongé, Jean-Zera's father, has now departed with the tide (Night 21). By burning the Giannis pipeline Jean-Zera had frozen Claude's antiquities smuggling; Claude gave him two nights, and Jean-Zera made good — the crypt relics crated and shipped (the Petrus Gyllius diary slipped out with the cargo), and at the quay a reverse parting-gift of a lesser book from Osman's shelves and a handful of catacomb coins. The legal antiquities line to the Padishah — Jean-Zera's sketchbook as catalogue — was pitched and provisionally accepted, but remains unproven: Claude will return in a few weeks to see whether it was real. The peace-letter to Giannis came back unopened; that bridge stays burned. New, minor: Chique Manu's own father came looking for Jean-Zera — a connection the bard is in no hurry to take up.

Loose dangers

Things left alive, loose, or unresolved that can resurface:

  • The half-freed Shahmeran. Two of four chains broken; mobile in her cell and able to cast, her "obligation" declared fulfilled. Contact runs through Faravi — an ancient serpentess now has a line into Sidra's own bond.
  • The palace mole. An unnamed official fled the peace talks after Kavalan's murder of Hamit; both the party and Mejid noticed.
  • Kavalan's leverage. He killed Hamit publicly and cast Speak with Dead — putting Jean-Zera's description on record and implicating the palace. Talks continue, strained.
  • Alexander's vendetta. He knows Ahra killed his wife Nadia for using magic. Set aside for now, but a powder keg under the Janissary–Sekban mediation the Ghost wants.
  • Kore's true nature — a Darkness-weaving shade — remains unknown to most of the party.

Faction pressure

  • The Ghost / Alemdar — his coup is rolling. At the Negotiation he declared it outright: a military coup, effected by his Sekban, already in motion and to proceed with the table or without it, toward an assembly of the people. He is stockpiling Greek fire for it — Sidra traced a cart from Papa Mert to his compound (Night 20) — and Papa Mert's compounding continues to feed the Sekban. The party leans to him as the least-bad option but refused to bless the coup. He is not seeking rulership for himself; whether Act Four's last fight is against Ahra, against Alemdar, or beside him now turns on how the party plays the weeks ahead.
  • Ahra "the Wolf" — now openly at war with the Ghost. At the table she scorned his council and he answered with contempt; the enmity is public. Her platform is the Ghost's mirror-image: the non-human confined below, the Janissary Hearth restored, Bergüzar's head on a spike and the Harem dissolved, a hard hand abroad, and the Padishah replaced by her pretender Bayezid. The buried fuse is still unlit: the Ghost is Alemdar Pasha, who killed her father at the greatest good — and she does not know it. The party knows; she does not.
  • Hano "the Widow" & Murad — left out, and so unwatched. The party deliberately did not invite the turned Widow or her anti-magic strongman Murad to the Negotiation — a clean choice that also means they have no read on what the pair are doing while every other power sat at the table. Hano still favours Murad over the Padishah, still runs hunters against suspected non-humans (Miray read the standing kill-order on her desk), and still has not clocked Miray herself. A live blind spot heading into the endgame.
  • A Certain Community / Aziz Sefa remains the live antagonist, though it did not surface at the Negotiation — no raid came, and the "derelict" tavern held. Aziz still knows the party has Mira, still has a man watching the wet-nurse Emine (Night 20), and the Padishah's search still tightens. He will surface again — and the palace infiltration runs straight through his watch.

Side errands still open

  • Crown of Sargon — claimed (Nights 17–18), attuned to Sidra, and still in the party's hands: not traded at the Negotiation. Sharif's standing offer is now explicit — the Crown in exchange for the secret of who told him the party shelters another magic-user (Mira); he asked them to bring it to the table and, mid-sitting, whispered Sidra into slipping it on beneath her scarf (to what end he did not say). The keep-it-or-spend-it decision (power in hand vs. the loyalty of the Council of the Ninth Life and their palace-spies) is still live, and its ties to the Ninth Life and the sick-cats web remain.
  • Alexander & Shehrazat — Alexander agreed to tutor Shehrazat if hidden from both Ahra and Alemdar; his short-term lodging at Papa Mert's has likely lapsed and needs a longer arrangement. Shehrazat was also told to seek Dr. Feuerbach at the Pera Palace Hotel for her unstable magic, but cannot go above ground herself.
  • The Spectral Hippodrome — Susanna and Chique Manu's race-fixing; a promised new race pending; the Blue Rider's departure has destabilized the Blue/Green balance.
  • The sick cats & red eggs — Sharif's unanswered request, knotted to Eve, the Ninth Life, and the Crown.
  • Antiquities & the mint — Giannis stays publicly burned (the peace-letter bounced), and Claude has now sailed on the legal Padishah-line pitch — provisionally accepted, unproven, to be tested when he returns in weeks. New and unexplored: Bedros is an assayer at the mint — a quiet lever over the realm's coinage that the party has noted and not yet pulled.
  • Deniz — met and half-won (Night 20). Miray found the goat-druid fighting in the Ermeydani, hailed her in Druidic, and reached a tentative accord: balance without subjugation or revenge. Deniz cares only for her oasis and is indifferent to who rules — unless a victor threatens the Undercity, which Eve does (she asked, pointedly, if Miray was "one of Eve's"). A first druid ally, and a live line on the Eve thread.
  • A friend inside the palace — Emine the wet-nurse (alias "Rena"). Referred to Gizem by Gülbahar, she is a covert, frightened Sufism-seeker who wants gentle wisdom to ease another distressed, pregnant lady of the harem (very possibly Bergüzar). Gizem's harem "in" runs through the pregnant concubine Gulnaz and the coffee-mistress Dilrûba. Danger: a spymaster's man tailed Emine home — A Certain Community is watching her.

Where it all points

The table is set and struck: the Negotiation is behind them, and it resolved the one thing the party could not talk their way around — the Ghost's coup is already in motion, and the only questions left are whether to steer it, break with it, or ride it out. They lean to Alemdar as the least-bad of three futures but have sworn to nothing, which leaves them, with three sessions to the end, needing to decide what they are actually for — and what Mira is for: a shield for the Haseki Sultan, a lever against her, or a fugitive kept quietly alive. From here the module drives toward The Eyes of Suleiman: the Ghost's plan is to sever the Padishah from the Harem by removing Bergüzar, but her visions — and now Mira herself, the sister who could talk her down or take her place — give the party the chance to propose sparing her instead, the campaign's moral hinge and the likeliest spine of the finale. That means reaching the palace (through Aziz's tightening watch, perhaps with the cats' help if the Crown is spent), before the Lunar Eclipse and one of the three endings (see The Acts). On the current lean, the last reckoning is with the Janissary side under Ahra — with a live chance that Ahra learns who the Ghost is, and the whole board tips.

☾ Next Session (proposed)

Proposal for Night 22 — into the palace (2026-09-27). The second of the last four sessions. The Negotiation is struck and the coup is rolling; this is the pivot — the party commits an aim and turns from the tavern toward the Harem. The through-line: reach Bergüzar before the eclipse, and decide whether to spare her or spend her. Options, not rails.

First: what are they for?

  • Force the commitment the Negotiation dodged. They lean to the Ghost but swore to nothing. Open the night by making them name the aim they will actually spend the last three sessions on — the recommended synthesis is protect Mira + avert the Wish by sparing Bergüzar (see her visions), steering the Ghost's coup toward the assembly and away from the head-on-a-spike. Everything below serves whichever aim they choose.
  • The coup is a clock now, not a question. Alemdar's Sekban are moving with or without them, fed by Papa Mert's Greek fire. Whatever the party does at the palace happens against that ticking — and a coup that goes loud while Mira sits a wall from Bergüzar's failing power is a disaster waiting to be timed.

Reaching the Harem — the ways in

  • Gizem's channel. The wet-nurse Emine (watched by Aziz's man), the coffee-mistress Dilrûba, and the pregnant concubine Gulnaz are the soft way in — and the harem was already "on edge," the Haseki Sultan visibly troubled, when the party last looked. Handle Emine as both asset and liability: a message carried inward, or a tail led to disaster.
  • The cats. If the party finally spends the Crown on Sharif, the Council of the Ninth Life can hand them palace-spies and interference — the cleanest counter to Aziz's watch. Sharif's price for the secret (who told him of Mira) is the same Crown, so the trade does double duty.
  • The mint lever. Bedros — assayer, Lodge remnant, "levers to pull" — is a wholly new angle: coinage, and a foot already inside sanctioned institutions. Untested, and his own agenda unknown.

The moral hinge — the sister-meeting

  • Mira ↔ Bergüzar. This is the heart of the endgame: the untrained sister who could talk the Haseki Sultan down, or take her place, or refuse both. Whether the party engineers a secret meeting — and what Mira wants, given she is markedly non-committal about reunion — decides whether "spare Bergüzar" is even on the table. Lay the ground here even if the meeting itself falls to Night 23.

Threads to weave in

  • The unlit fuse. Ahra now openly opposes the Ghost and does not know he killed her father. Any scene that puts her near the truth (a loose word, an old comrade, the Lodge's records of the greatest good) can tip the whole board — hold it, or light it, deliberately.
  • The blind spot. Hano and Murad were left out and unwatched. Let them act off-screen — a raid, a claim staked, "Ahmet" clocked — so the choice to skip them has a cost.
  • The Lodge initiation. Jean-Zera still owes the winged initiate the right answer; the scrolls, Osman's papers, or Faravi can teach it. The reward is the Lodge itself, and a pocket-study hiding place for Mira. The blind ~6×6 ft wall by Osman's kitchen is still unopened.
  • Aziz surfaces. He did not disrupt the Negotiation; he is overdue. The palace approach is exactly where A Certain Community's watch and the Padishah's search converge — crystallize Aziz as the finale's near-enemy here.

Where it should land: a committed aim, a route into the Harem opened (cats bought or the harem channel worked), the sister-meeting seeded, and Aziz squarely in the way — with two sessions left to reach Bergüzar and the eclipse. If the party stalls, the coup's clock and the Sultan's worsening distress force the issue: the raids widen, and events at the palace start happening to them.
